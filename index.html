Complex system note
Push and pull factors don’t act alone—they amplify or cancel each other.
Explanation
In a complex system, no single factor “causes” migration. Instead, conflict, economic collapse, and rights restrictions combine to create intense pressure to leave. At the same time, Australia’s safety, jobs, and services create a strong pull—but only for those who can access visas, routes, or networks.

Migration Journey
From Afghanistan to Australia, risk and opportunity change at each step.
Timeline
Departure from Afghanistan
High push, uncertain pull
Families weigh danger at home against the unknown abroad. Networks, savings, and smugglers often shape who can leave.
Transit countries
Risk, waiting, and bureaucracy
Migrants may spend months or years in transit, facing detention, informal work, or refugee status determination.
Arrival in Australia
New safety, new barriers
Visa status, language, and recognition of qualifications shape how quickly migrants can access work and services.
Settlement & belonging
Long-term integration
Over time, migrants build communities, send remittances, and influence both Afghan and Australian societies.

Complexity note
Migration is not just “refugees” – it’s also about skills and economies.
Context
Australia’s migration system often distinguishes between humanitarian and skilled migrants, but in reality many Afghans are both: they flee danger and bring valuable skills.
You can use this card to argue that migration is not only a “problem” to be managed, but also a way Australia responds to labour shortages, especially in health, regional areas, and critical infrastructure.
